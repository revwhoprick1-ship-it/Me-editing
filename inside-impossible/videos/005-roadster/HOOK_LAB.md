# Hook lab — #005 "What if every car was the Tesla Roadster?" (scientific method)

## 1. Observation
- User verdict on #004: "Hook sucks." #004 opened with a question ("How many times would you have to spit…") over a slow
  push on a mostly still wide shot, in a robotic local TTS voice.
- Our own analytics (vault/02 Style Guide/Jorho style.md):
  - motion in the first 0.6 s (Jorho box intro) → 72 % stayed-to-watch vs 55–58 % for the same edits without it;
  - a recognisable name in second 1 (v19 "TREVOR LAWRENCE · NFL QB") held; v24 swapped it for a vague label and flatlined;
  - a concrete bold claim top line (v25 "$20,000 to $500 in 3 seconds") beat a vague, news-like one (v24);
  - the version with an AI voice-over got 28 % → voice quality is part of the hook.

## 2. Background (peer-reviewed, why these levers should work)
- Information-gap theory: curiosity spikes when a *specific* gap is made salient (Loewenstein 1994, *Psychological
  Bulletin* 116:75–98). A vague "what if?" names no gap; "here's what breaks first" does.
- Curiosity recruits reward circuitry and is strongest for answers people feel they *almost* know (Kang et al. 2009,
  *Psychological Science* 20:963–973) → familiar objects (mom's minivan, a Tesla) + an unknown outcome.
- Bayesian surprise attracts gaze within a fraction of a second (Itti & Baldi 2009, *Vision Research* 49:1295–1306) →
  frame 0 must contain an unexpected, fast change, not a slow establishing shot.

## 3. Hypothesis
A hook that (a) starts with large motion at frame 0, (b) names a recognisable thing within 1.5 s, (c) states an absurd
premise as *already happened* with a vivid personal image ("your mom's minivan"), and (d) opens an explicit loop
("here's what breaks first") will lose fewer viewers in 0–3 s than a plain "What if…?" question over a still frame.

## 4. Experiment (pre-publication): six candidates scored 0–2 on seven criteria
Criteria: M motion at frame 0 · R recognisable name ≤ 1.5 s · C concrete/vivid in sentence 1 · P personal ("you/your")
· L open loop · S spoken ≤ 3.5 s · V visual = words (the first image IS the sentence).

| # | Hook | M | R | C | P | L | S | V | Σ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (control) | "What if every car on Earth was a Tesla Roadster?" | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 1 | 6 |
| B | "Every car on Earth just became Tesla's new flying Roadster, and it does 0–60 in 0.8 seconds." | 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 8 |
| C | "1.5 billion cars just turned into Tesla Roadsters. Here's the first thing that breaks." | 2 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 9 |
| D | "Tesla's new Roadster can flip upside down and fly. Now imagine every car is one." | 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 10 |
| E | "Your mom's minivan just turned into a Tesla Roadster." | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 12 |
| **F (chosen)** | "Your mom's minivan just turned into a Tesla Roadster. So did every car on Earth. Here's what breaks first." | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2 | **13** |

Viewer simulation (cold, sound on): A → "ok, and?" (swipe risk high, no image yet); F → a minivan *explodes* into a red
Roadster in frame 0 while Max jumps back, then "here's what breaks first" makes the next 3 s a promise. F kept.

## 5. Visual half of the hook (frame 0 rules)
- Frame 0 already moving: minivan mid-transform (panels flying, sparks), camera whip-in; Max mid-jump with a shocked face.
- Recognition: "TESLA ROADSTER" label + the car's silhouette by 1.0 s; chip "EVERY CAR ON EARTH" at the second sentence.
- No title card, no logo, no slow push.

## 6. How we will measure (after upload)
- Primary: YouTube Studio → "Stayed to watch" vs "Swiped away" for #005 vs #004 and #002 after 48 h.
- Secondary: audience-retention graph at 3 s.
- Confounds (honest): this video also changes the voice, the mascot and the topic, so a win can't be credited to the
  hook alone. To isolate it later: re-cut #004 with only the new hook pattern and compare.
